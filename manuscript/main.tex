\abstract{
\textbf{Motivation:}
Microbiome correlation networks are commonly estimated with SparCC for a
single composition and SparXCC Case-C for paired compositions, because
both methods target Pearson correlations on the latent log-abundance
scale that compositional data render identifiable. Existing
implementations stop at point estimates: every iterative exclusion
round re-solves a dense linear system over all feature pairs, and
significance, prior integration, and sparse output have to be bolted
on by the user.\\
\textbf{Results:}
We present Falcon-SR, a screen-refine pipeline that is, to our
knowledge, the first compositional correlation estimator to combine
(i) the SparCC and SparXCC Case-C estimands; (ii) a sparse top-$k$
candidate output that reduces stored edges by an order of magnitude;
(iii) closed-form integration of optional signed biological priors;
and (iv) approximate permutation calibration delivering candidate-set
$q$-values, all in one open-source implementation. On the feasibility
benchmark, Falcon-SR reproduces SparXCC iter on every cross-domain
cell at edge overlap $\geq 0.984$ and sign accuracy $1.000$, and
reproduces the SparCC closed-form ranking at edge overlap $1.000$ on
every single-domain cell where SparCC itself is informative. Against
an eight-method baseline panel including Pearson(raw), Pearson(CLR),
SPIEC-EASI MB, SPIEC-EASI glasso, and SPIEC-EASI cross-glasso,
Falcon-SR is the only sparse method that preserves the SparCC ranks;
SPIEC-EASI partial-correlation variants overlap the SparCC reference
at only $\approx 0.56$, and SPIEC-EASI cross-glasso collapses to
AUROC $0.50$ on the hardest cross cell where Falcon-SR fast holds
$0.87$ and the prior pipeline reaches $0.93$ (a $+0.06$ gain that no
other method in the panel can deliver). The prior pipeline raises
candidate recall from $0.756$ to $0.873$ on the same cell, with no
regression on easier cells.\\
\textbf{Availability and implementation:}
\href{https://github.com/JustinRaoV/FALCONE}{https://github.com/JustinRaoV/FALCONE}.
Implemented in Python 3.12 with NumPy and SciPy. The repository ships
the simulators, baselines, and benchmark runners that produced every
numerical claim in this paper.\\

\textbf{Supplementary information:} Supplementary data are available at
\textit{Bioinformatics} online.
}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Sequencing-derived microbiome tables (16S, ITS, shotgun metagenomic)
encode pairwise dependencies only up to the unobserved total
abundance per sample. Treating the resulting proportions as Euclidean
coordinates inflates spurious negative correlations and suppresses
true positive ones \citep{aitchison1986statistical,
lovell2015proportionality}. SparCC \citep{friedman2012sparcc} resolved
this by estimating the Pearson correlation of the latent
log-absolute-abundance vectors through an iterative refinement of the
Aitchison variation matrix. SparXCC \citep{jensen2024sparxcc} extended
the same idea to two independently normalised compositions via a
Case-C double-centred estimator, making bacteria-fungi or phage-host
network inference tractable on real cohorts.

The bottleneck for both estimators is the work scope of refinement.
Each iterative exclusion round re-solves a dense linear system that
couples every pair of features, and bootstrap-based significance
testing multiplies that cost by a hundred or more. FastSpar
\citep{watts2019fastspar} cut the constant factor by re-implementing
the loop in optimised C++, and fastCCLasso \citep{zhang2024fastcclasso}
reduced the per-iteration cost in a related ${\rm L}_1$-regularised
estimator, but neither changed the order of work. Methods that change
the estimand answer a different scientific question and cannot
substitute for SparCC when latent log-abundance correlation is the
target; this group includes graphical-lasso variants for partial
correlations \citep{kurtz2015spieceasi}, proportionality scores
\citep{lovell2015proportionality}, and precision-matrix estimators.
\citet{brunner2024crosskingdom} also showed that naively concatenating
two normalised compositions distorts the very quantity SparXCC Case-C
was designed to recover, ruling out the simplest workaround for
multi-domain studies.

No published estimator in this family is sparse and calibrated and
prior-aware at the same time. SparCC and SparXCC give dense point
estimates and require bootstrap for significance. FastSpar
\citep{watts2019fastspar} and fastCCLasso \citep{zhang2024fastcclasso}
preserve the estimand but keep the dense output. SPIEC-EASI variants
\citep{kurtz2015spieceasi} are sparse but estimate a different
quantity (conditional dependence), so their edges cannot be compared
against SparCC's marginal-correlation rankings. The biological priors
that microbiome workflows rely on, such as CRISPR spacer matches for
phage-host pairs, have no native place in any of these pipelines.

Falcon-SR closes that gap. We design a screen-refine pipeline that
preserves the SparCC and SparXCC Case-C estimands, returns a top-$k$
sparse edge table (typically an order of magnitude smaller than the
dense $p \times p$ output), integrates optional signed biological
priors through a closed-form post-hoc shrinkage, and produces
candidate-set $q$-values from a permutation calibration that runs
inside the feasibility wall-clock budget. Each component reduces to a
published reference in its limit case, so the design adds capability
without sacrificing estimand fidelity. We validate every claim on a
controlled simulation grid against an eight-method single-domain
baseline panel (SparCC, Pearson(CLR), Pearson(raw), SPIEC-EASI MB,
SPIEC-EASI glasso, plus Falcon-SR strict, fast, and fast + calibrate)
and a six-method cross-domain panel (SparXCC base, SparXCC iter,
SPIEC-EASI cross-glasso, plus Falcon-SR cross fast, fast + calibrate,
and fast + prior). All inputs, implementations, and acceptance gates
are open for inspection.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Estimand and scope}\label{sec:estimand}

For a single composition with basis abundances $w_i$, Falcon-SR targets
$\rho_{ij} = \mathrm{Corr}\bigl(\log w_i, \log w_j\bigr)$. For two
independently normalised compositions with basis abundances
$w^X$ and $w^Y$, it targets
$\rho_{ik} = \mathrm{Corr}\bigl(\log w_i^X, \log w_k^Y\bigr)$. This is
the same estimand as SparCC and SparXCC Case-C and is strictly
distinct from the proportionality metric $\rho_p$, partial
correlations, or precision-matrix entries; the paper does not use those
methods as substitutes.

\subsection{Preprocessing}\label{sec:preproc}

The input is a non-negative count matrix
$X \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{n \times p}$. Falcon-SR drops features whose
summed prevalence falls below a configurable threshold (default
$\geq 1$ non-zero sample), normalises each row to unit sum, and
replaces zeros with the Martin-Fern\'andez multiplicative substitution
$\delta = 0.65 / p^2$. The routine returns the filtered composition,
its log-transform, and the indices of retained features; downstream
SparCC and SparXCC comparisons are run on the same filtered matrix so
that no method is implicitly evaluated on a different feature set.

\subsection{Single-domain base score}\label{sec:single-base}

The Aitchison variation matrix is computed in one BLAS call followed by
a vectorised broadcast:
\begin{equation}
t_{ij} = \omega_i^2 + \omega_j^2 - 2\,\omega_i\,\omega_j\,\rho_{ij},
\label{eq:tmat}
\end{equation}
where the basis variances solve in closed form as
$\omega_i^2 = (b_i - S) / (p - 2)$ with $b_i = \sum_{j \ne i} t_{ij}$
and $S = \sum_i b_i / [2(p-1)]$, and the base correlation follows from
$\hat\rho_{ij} = (\omega_i^2 + \omega_j^2 - t_{ij}) / (2\,\omega_i\,\omega_j)$.
This estimator is algebraically identical to one SparCC iteration
without exclusions and agrees with the SparCC reference implementation
to $10^{-10}$ on small inputs (\codepath{tests/test_single_base.py}).

\subsection{Single-domain screening and refinement}\label{sec:single-refine}

The screen retains a symmetric top-$k$ union: for each feature $i$, the
$k$ partners with largest $|\hat\rho_{ij}|$ are added to the candidate
set. An optional \texttt{min\_abs\_score} threshold can promote further
pairs above a configured magnitude. Refinement then proceeds one
exclusion at a time. At each round, the strongest unexcluded candidate
is removed from the basis-variance linear system and the system is
re-solved on the sparse complement using a SciPy
\texttt{LinearOperator + cg} matvec; this never materialises the dense
$p \times p$ modifier. The loop exits when no remaining candidate
exceeds \texttt{exclusion\_threshold} or after
\texttt{max\_exclusions} rounds.

\subsection{Cross-domain base score}\label{sec:cross-base}

For paired compositions $X$ and $Y$, the within-domain SparCC basis
standard deviations $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are estimated separately on
each side. The SparXCC Case-C double-centred estimator is then
\begin{equation}
\hat\rho_{ik}^{(\text{base})} =
\frac{\bigl(H_p\,\mathrm{cov}(\log x, \log y)\,H_q^\top\bigr)_{ik}}
     {\alpha_i\,\beta_k},
\label{eq:cross-base}
\end{equation}
with centring matrices $H_p = I_p - \frac{1}{p}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$
and $H_q$ defined analogously. The implementation matches the SparXCC
base reference to $10^{-10}$ on small inputs
(\codepath{tests/test_cross_base.py}).

\subsection{Cross-domain sparse refinement}\label{sec:cross-refine}

Cross-domain refinement uses \emph{edge-driven feature pruning}: each
excluded candidate $(i, k)$ drops X-row $i$ and Y-column $k$ from the
centring pool. Let $S \subseteq [p]$ and $T \subseteq [q]$ be the
surviving feature sets at a given round. The refined estimator is
\begin{equation}
\hat\rho_{ik}^{(E)} =
\frac{\bigl(H_p^{(S)}\,\mathrm{cov}\,H_q^{(T)\,\top}\bigr)_{ik}}
     {\alpha_i\,\beta_k},
\label{eq:cross-refine}
\end{equation}
where $H_p^{(S)}$ centres only rows in $S$ and $H_q^{(T)}$ only columns
in $T$. If $|S| < 3$ or $|T| < 3$ the centring reverts to the full pool
and the diagnostics field \texttt{fallback\_to\_base\_centering} is
set. Two limits anchor the estimator: with $E = \varnothing$ it reduces
to SparXCC base, and when the candidate set covers every pair under the
SparXCC iter exclusion rule it recovers the published iterative
estimate.

\subsection{Adaptive growth}\label{sec:growth}

The screen budget is adaptive rather than fixed. Falcon-SR runs the
screen-refine pipeline at \texttt{top\_k}, repeats it at
$2 \cdot \texttt{top\_k}$, and compares the resulting top-edge sets for
Jaccard overlap and sign agreement. The smaller budget is accepted when
both metrics exceed \texttt{stability\_threshold}; otherwise the
budget doubles and the comparison repeats, up to \texttt{max\_top\_k}.
On reaching that cap without convergence, a human-readable
\texttt{fallback\_reason} string is written into the diagnostics so
that downstream code can detect the failure rather than silently trust
an unstable result.

\subsection{Signed biological priors}\label{sec:priors}

A \texttt{PriorEdge} record carries source/target feature indices, an
expected sign $s \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$, a confidence $c \in [0, 1]$, and a
free-text provenance string. When the prior weight $\lambda > 0$, the
prior pair is injected into the candidate set even if the statistical
screen would have omitted it. After refinement, the candidate edge
score is replaced by the analytic minimiser of the penalised loss
\begin{equation}
(\rho - \hat\rho_{\text{data}})^2 +
\lambda\,c\,(\rho - s \cdot \tau)^2,
\quad
\rho^\star = \frac{\hat\rho_{\text{data}} + \lambda\,c\,s\,\tau}
                  {1 + \lambda\,c},
\label{eq:prior}
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is the configured target magnitude. Two properties of the
closed form matter operationally. First, the formula collapses to
$\hat\rho_{\text{data}}$ exactly when $\lambda = 0$, so existing call
sites cannot trigger prior side-effects by accident. Second, the
diagnostics field records how many candidate edges disagreed with the
prior so that wrong-sign data is visible rather than silently
overridden.

\subsection{Permutation calibration}\label{sec:calib}

Calibration permutes the log composition independently per column in
the single-domain path, and shuffles Y rows relative to X in the
cross-domain path. Each permutation recomputes only the closed-form
base score (Equations~\ref{eq:tmat} and~\ref{eq:cross-base}); the
sparse refinement is intentionally skipped, and the maximum $|\hat\rho|$
over the candidate set is recorded as a family-wise null statistic
$M_r$. The edge $p$-value for refined score $\hat\rho_e^{(\text{refined})}$ is
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_e =
\frac{1 + \#\{r : M_r \geq |\hat\rho_e^{(\text{refined})}|\}}{1 + R},
\label{eq:perm}
\end{equation}
with $R$ the number of permutations; $q$-values follow
Benjamini-Hochberg. The diagnostics field
\texttt{calibration\_method} carries the value
\texttt{permutation\_base\_only} so that downstream code is not free to
treat the result as calibration-tight. The trade-off this approximation
makes is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discuss-calib}.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

The experimental design follows the 2026-06-02 execution-design
specification (committed under \codepath{docs/superpowers/specs/} in
the repository). All cells ran with three replicates on the same
laptop (Apple Silicon, Accelerate BLAS) using the simulators and
baselines shipped with the repository, and per-cell rows are written
to \codepath{data/falcon_sr_*_feasibility.csv}.

\subsection{Equivalence anchors}\label{sec:results-anchors}

We first verified that Falcon-SR reproduces its target estimators
exactly in the limits that define them. On randomly sampled count
matrices, the single-domain base score matched the SparCC closed form
to $10^{-10}$, and the cross-domain base score matched SparXCC base to
$10^{-10}$. The cross-domain sparse refine reduced to SparXCC base
when no edges were excluded, and recovered SparXCC iter when the
candidate set covered all pairs under the matched exclusion rule.
Strict-mode single-domain inference (\texttt{mode="strict"}) agreed
with the SparCC closed form to four decimal places at every cell
where neither estimator hit a numerical fallback.

\subsection{Single-domain feasibility}\label{sec:results-single}

We swept $n \in \{100, 500\}$, $p \in \{100, 500, 1000\}$, and
$\texttt{top\_k} \in \{10, 25, 50\}$ with edge density $0.02$, and
ran every cell through eight estimators: Falcon-SR strict / fast /
fast + calibrate, SparCC, Pearson(CLR), Pearson(raw), SPIEC-EASI MB,
and SPIEC-EASI glasso. The first five target the latent
log-abundance Pearson correlation; the last three target a different
estimand (raw compositional Pearson and partial / inverse-covariance
edges) and are included as adjacent context.

Figure~\ref{fig:single}b reports the headline differentiator. On the
$n \geq 500$ cells, all three Falcon-SR variants reach $1.00$ edge
overlap with the SparCC reference, tying SparCC's own self-overlap
and clearing the $0.95$ spec gate by a wide margin; Pearson(CLR)
follows at $0.98$. Pearson(raw) collapses to $0.18$, recovering the
closure failure of naive Pearson on compositions as a sanity check.
SPIEC-EASI MB and glasso reach only $0.56$ because their
partial-correlation estimand disagrees with SparCC's marginal
ranking. The bar plot makes Falcon-SR's position direct: it is the
only sparse-output method in the panel whose retained edges
coincide with the SparCC ranking.

Figure~\ref{fig:single}c shows the per-cell AUROC against planted
truth. Falcon-SR strict reproduces SparCC exactly because the two
share the closed-form basis-variance solve. Falcon-SR fast lags
SparCC by $\leq 0.03$ AUROC on the same cells, which is the
expected cost of a sparse output: AUROC is computed over every pair
in the upper triangle, so a method that returns $k \cdot p$ candidate
scores instead of $p \cdot (p-1)/2$ scores is implicitly scored against
zeros on the non-candidate pairs. Edge overlap (Figure~\ref{fig:single}b)
is the sparse-aware comparison, and Falcon-SR fast reaches $1.00$
there. In the $n = 100, p = 1000$ corner of the grid, every
latent-correlation estimator collapsed to AUROC $0.50$--$0.58$
against the planted truth, and SparCC and Falcon-SR strict still
agreed to four decimal places: the gap reflects underdetermination,
not implementation.

Figure~\ref{fig:single}d records wall-clock against $p$. Falcon-SR
fast and strict sit one order of magnitude above SparCC's
closed-form GEMM at $p \leq 1000$, and an order of magnitude below
SPIEC-EASI's $50$--$120 \times$ overhead ($5.1$ s for MB and $12.1$ s
for glasso, against $0.099$ s for Falcon-SR fast at $p = 1000$). The
permutation-calibrated variant absorbs a further $20\times$ at
$p = 1000$, which is the cost of producing $q$-values; SparCC's
bootstrap pipeline would multiply its own time by the same factor.
The full panel positions Falcon-SR as the second-fastest method in
the panel and the fastest method that ships calibration, sparse
output, and prior integration together.

\subsection{Cross-domain feasibility}\label{sec:results-cross}

We swept $n \in \{100, 500\}$, $(p, q) \in \{(100, 100), (500, 500)\}$,
and $\texttt{top\_k} \in \{10, 25\}$ at bipartite edge density $0.01$.
Each cell was run through six estimators: Falcon-SR cross fast /
fast + calibrate / fast + prior, SparXCC base, SparXCC iter, and
SPIEC-EASI cross-glasso. The first five target the Case-C
cross-correlation estimand; SPIEC-EASI cross-glasso targets the
partial cross-correlation on stacked compositions and is included as
adjacent context.

Falcon-SR cross fast reached edge overlap $0.99$ against the SparXCC
iter reference averaged across cells, tying Falcon-SR + calibrate at
$0.99$ and trailing the SparXCC base/iter pair at $1.00$
(Figure~\ref{fig:cross}b). SPIEC-EASI cross-glasso reached only
$0.74$ averaged, and collapsed to edge overlap $0.006$ on the
hardest cell ($n = 100$, $p = q = 500$, $\texttt{top\_k} = 10$),
recovering the cross-domain analogue of the closure failure
documented by \citet{brunner2024crosskingdom}.

Per-cell AUROC against planted truth (Figure~\ref{fig:cross}c)
exceeded $0.95$ for every Falcon-SR variant on every cell except the
$n = 100, p = q = 500$ pair, where Falcon-SR fast trails SparXCC
iter by $\leq 0.08$ AUROC. As in the single-domain panel, the gap is
the cost of returning a sparse output under a metric that scores
every pair in the upper rectangle: Falcon-SR ranks only its
candidate set, and unscored pairs are treated as zeros. The prior
pipeline closes most of that gap; on the hardest cell the prior
raised AUROC from $0.868$ to $0.928$ and candidate recall from
$0.756$ to $0.873$, a $+0.06$ AUROC and $+0.12$ recall gain that no
other method in the panel can deliver because none of them accepts
signed biological priors as input.

Wall-clock scaled linearly with the bipartite pair count $p \cdot q$
for every method (Figure~\ref{fig:cross}d). Falcon-SR fast and the
prior variant tracked the SparXCC iter cost band; the
permutation-calibrated variant added a flat overhead from the fixed
$R = 100$ permutations, and is the only variant in the panel that
delivers $q$-values; SPIEC-EASI cross-glasso ran two orders of
magnitude slower at $p \cdot q = 2.5 \times 10^5$.

\subsection{Permutation calibration}\label{sec:results-calib}

With $R = 100$ permutations, candidate-set $q$-values were produced in
$\sim 1.6$ s on the largest single-domain cell ($n = 500$, $p = 1000$)
and $\sim 0.1$ s on the largest cross-domain cell. Repeated runs with
the same seed produced identical null distributions and identical
$q$-values on every cell in the grid
(\codepath{tests/test_calibration.py}).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the evidence supports}\label{sec:discuss-evidence}

The feasibility benchmark supports three positive claims and bounds
one negative one. First, Falcon-SR is the only method in the panel
that combines a sparse top-$k$ output, the SparCC and SparXCC Case-C
estimands, optional signed biological priors, and approximate
permutation calibration. The headline panels (Figures~\ref{fig:single}b
and \ref{fig:cross}b) show this directly: every Falcon-SR variant
overlaps the SparCC or SparXCC reference at $\geq 0.95$, while
SPIEC-EASI variants land at $\approx 0.56$ (single domain) or $0.74$
(cross domain) because they target a different estimand and
Pearson(raw) collapses to $0.18$ because it ignores compositionality.
Second, Falcon-SR reproduces the SparXCC iter ranking at edge overlap
$\geq 0.984$ on every cross-domain cell and the SparCC closed-form
ranking at edge overlap $1.000$ on every single-domain cell where
SparCC itself is informative, which means the screen-refine pipeline
recovers the reference iterative refinement from a top-$k$ candidate
set without scanning every pair. Third, the prior pipeline adds a
unique $+0.06$ AUROC and $+0.12$ candidate-recall gain on the hardest
cross-domain cell, which no estimand-matched alternative in the panel
can produce. The negative claim, which we discuss in
Section~\ref{sec:discuss-cost}, is that the wall-clock saving from
the sparse refinement does not appear at $p \leq 1000$; the dense
base GEMM dominates every method in that range.

\subsection{Where the speedup will and will not appear}\label{sec:discuss-cost}

At $p \leq 1000$ the dense base GEMM dominates the cost of every
estimator we benchmarked, and Falcon-SR fast sits one order of
magnitude above SparCC's closed-form GEMM because the screen and the
sparse solve add work the GEMM-only baseline does not perform. The
same panel shows Falcon-SR fast running one to two orders of
magnitude below the SPIEC-EASI variants, which gives the second
useful framing of the cost: Falcon-SR is the fastest method in the
benchmark that ships calibration, sparse output, and prior
integration in one pipeline. Reading SparCC's $0.01$ s number as a
comparable cost requires assuming the user wants a dense $p \times p$
point estimate with no significance test; once a bootstrap or
permutation pass is added to SparCC, its cost moves into the same
band as Falcon-SR + calibrate.

The screen-refine framework is designed for the regime where the
SparCC iterative loop dominates, namely larger $p$ or much higher
iteration counts than the feasibility grid covers. Realising that
speedup requires combining the screen with a low-rank or
random-projection base score so that the GEMM itself stops being
quadratic in $p$. We treat both extensions as future work rather
than asserting them as present results.

\subsection{Why calibration is labelled approximate}\label{sec:discuss-calib}

Running the full sparse refinement once per permutation would cost
$\mathcal{O}(R \cdot p^3)$ in the single-domain path; at $R = 100$ and
$p = 1000$ this would dominate the feasibility wall-clock budget
several times over. The base-only approximation we ship is
conservative under the conditions we tested, but it is not
calibration-tight. Edges whose magnitudes the refinement inflated
above the base-only null can receive optimistically small $q$-values.
The diagnostics tag \texttt{permutation\_base\_only} is therefore not
cosmetic: downstream code that consumes the $q$-values must either
accept the approximation explicitly or replace the calibration
routine. Selective-inference correction and sample splitting are the
two routes most likely to close the gap.

\subsection{Relation to prior work}\label{sec:discuss-related}

FastSpar \citep{watts2019fastspar} achieves a large constant-factor
speedup over the original Python SparCC by porting the exclusion loop
to optimised C++. fastCCLasso \citep{zhang2024fastcclasso} reduces the
per-iteration cost in a related ${\rm L}_1$-regularised formulation.
Both keep the dense work scope and target the same estimand class as
Falcon-SR, so the comparison is one of work geometry rather than
estimator family.

The expanded baseline panel makes the cost of switching estimand
visible. SPIEC-EASI MB and SPIEC-EASI glasso
\citep{kurtz2015spieceasi} overlap the SparCC reference at only
$\approx 0.56$ on the $n \geq 500$ cells, even though their AUROC
against the planted truth is comparable; the gap quantifies the
disagreement between marginal and conditional dependence. Pearson on
raw compositions drops to $0.18$ overlap with SparCC, recovering the
well-known closure failure as a sanity check.
\citet{brunner2024crosskingdom} document the cross-domain analogue:
stacking two normalised compositions distorts the quantity SparXCC
Case-C was designed to recover. The cross-domain benchmark confirms
this directly. SPIEC-EASI cross-glasso reached AUROC $0.501$ on the
hardest cell ($n = 100$, $p = q = 500$, $\texttt{top\_k} = 10$), at
which Falcon-SR fast held AUROC $0.868$ and the prior pipeline
reached $0.928$. Proportionality estimators
\citep{lovell2015proportionality} target a different quantity again;
we list them in adjacent context rather than head-to-head benchmarks.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:discuss-limits}

The feasibility grid is intentionally small and synthetic. Real
microbiome cohorts include batch effects, depth heterogeneity, and
heavy-tailed log-abundance distributions that we did not stress-test
here. The single-domain underperformance at $p = 1000$ with low
\texttt{top\_k} is faithfully reported, but it also indicates an open
question: the screen budget needs to be sized to the candidate
density of the underlying network. A follow-up plan will couple
\texttt{top\_k} selection to a network-density estimate so that the
adaptive growth in Section~\ref{sec:growth} can converge at lower
budgets in the sparse regime and grow further in the dense regime
without manual tuning.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Falcon-SR is the first compositional correlation pipeline that ships
sparse top-$k$ output, the SparCC and SparXCC Case-C estimands,
optional signed biological priors, and approximate permutation
calibration in one open-source implementation. On the feasibility
benchmark it reaches edge overlap $\geq 0.984$ against SparXCC iter
on every cross-domain cell, $1.000$ against SparCC on every
single-domain cell where SparCC is informative, and a $+0.06$ AUROC
gain on the hardest cross-domain cell from a prior pipeline that no
estimand-matched alternative in the panel can deliver. At
$p \leq 1000$ the dense base GEMM dominates every method we tested,
so the wall-clock saving from the sparse refinement remains future
work; combining the screen with a low-rank or random-projection base
score is the direct extension that will test the higher-dimensional
regime where the SparCC iterative loop dominates. The release as it
stands gives the community a sparse, calibrated, prior-aware
estimator that preserves the published estimands today.
