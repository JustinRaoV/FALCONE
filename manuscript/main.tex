\begin{abstract}
\noindent
\textbf{Motivation.}
Inferring correlation networks from compositional microbiome data is central to multi-omic studies. \sparcc{}~\cite{Friedman2012} is the standard estimator; \fastspar{}~\cite{Watts2019} accelerates it 32$\times$ via C++/OpenMP; \spieceasi{}~\cite{Kurtz2015} provides a graphical-lasso alternative; \sparxcc{}~\cite{SparXCC2024} extends the SparCC architecture to cross-domain correlations between two compositions. All retain a $B\!\cdot\!I \approx 1{,}000$ bootstrap-plus-iteration multiplier, none admit a biological prior, and the cross-domain method has no convex formulation.

\noindent
\textbf{Results.}
\falcon{} is a Python framework with three convex algorithms. \fastprop{} matches \sparcc{}, \spieceasi{}-glasso, and Pearson-on-CLR within $\pm 0.01$ AUROC on a shared planted-edge simulator across $p \in \{500,\ldots,5{,}000\}$, while delivering a $\sim 3\times$ Recall@$K$ improvement at $p\!=\!1{,}000$ via Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage. The $\sim 1{,}000\times$ algorithmic speedup over \sparcc{} (eliminating the bootstrap multiplier through an analytic Fisher-$z$ test) is complementary to \fastspar{}'s implementation speedup and is intrinsic to the algorithm. \crossnet{} replaces \sparxcc{}'s per-pair iterative correction with the closed-form matrix identity $T = H_p\,\Omega\,H_q^{\!\top}$, solved as a convex $\ell_1$-regularised inverse problem with provable $O(1/k^2)$ FISTA convergence and a signed biological prior $\Omega_{\mathrm{prior}}$ (e.g.\ CRISPR-spacer matches). On a planted phage--bacteria simulation at $p\!=\!q\!=\!500$, \crossnet{} cuts the false-positive rate by $\sim 25\%$ relative to \sparxcc{} and \spieceasi{}-cross (0.20 $\to$ 0.15).

\section{Introduction}

Microbiome studies routinely seek to reconstruct interaction networks from operational taxonomic unit (OTU) abundances. Sequencing data are compositional: only relative, not absolute, abundances are observed, and the unit-sum constraint biases naive Pearson correlations~\cite{Aitchison1986,Friedman2012,Gloor2017}. The Case-A problem (correlations within one composition) has three classes of published estimator: \sparcc{}~\cite{Friedman2012} works in log-ratio space and obtains per-pair p-values via a bootstrap of $B\!\approx\!100$ replicates wrapped around $I\!\approx\!10$ iterative-exclusion rounds, giving an $O(B I p^2 n)$ inner cost; \fastspar{}~\cite{Watts2019} reimplements this in C++ with OpenMP for a $32\times$ single-thread ($221\times$ on 16 threads) speedup without changing the asymptotic complexity; and \spieceasi{}~\cite{Kurtz2015} replaces the SparCC inner loop with neighborhood-selection or graphical-lasso estimation of the inverse covariance, returning partial correlations rather than marginal ones.

Multi-omic studies introduce a second problem (Case C): inferring correlations between two independently normalised compositional datasets, e.g.\ phage $X \in \Delta^{p-1}$ versus bacteria $Y \in \Delta^{q-1}$. The recent \sparxcc{}~\cite{SparXCC2024} extends \sparcc{}'s architecture to this case via a per-pair sample-mean correction with iterative thresholding, inheriting the same bootstrap-plus-iteration multiplier. No published method integrates external biological priors -- e.g.\ CRISPR-spacer-predicted host--phage links~\cite{iPHoP2023} -- as a soft constraint.

We present \falcon{}, a Python framework that addresses both problems through convex algorithms. (i)~\fastprop{} replaces the SparCC inner loop with a single BLAS GEMM ($O(np^2)$), Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage~\cite{LedoitWolf2004} and an analytic Fisher-$z$ test, eliminating the $B\!\cdot\!I\!=\!1{,}000$ multiplier algorithmically rather than via implementation acceleration. (ii)~\randprop{} extends inference to $p>10^4$ via Johnson--Lindenstrauss random projection~\cite{Achlioptas2003}. (iii)~\crossnet{} formulates the cross-domain bias as the closed-form matrix identity $T = H_p\,\Omega\,H_q^{\!\top}$ and recovers $\Omega$ as the solution of an $\ell_1$-regularised convex inverse problem with provable $O(1/k^2)$ FISTA convergence~\cite{BeckTeboulle2009,Nesterov1983}, optionally anchored by a signed prior $\Omega_{\mathrm{prior}}$.

\section{Implementation}

The three algorithms are implemented in $\sim 800$ lines of Python (NumPy $\geq$ 1.24, SciPy $\geq$ 1.10). \fastprop{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_domain}a) returns the proportionality matrix $\rho_p(i,j) = 2\hat\Sigma_{ij}/(\hat\Sigma_{ii}+\hat\Sigma_{jj})$~\cite{Lovell2015,Quinn2017} from a single BLAS GEMM on the shrunken CLR covariance; p-values follow analytically from $z_{ij} = \mathrm{arctanh}(\rho_p) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, (n-3)^{-1})$ with Benjamini--Hochberg FDR control~\cite{BenjaminiHochberg1995}. \randprop{} replaces the dense $p \times p$ matrix with an Achlioptas sparse projection into $d = O(\log p / \varepsilon^2)$ dimensions and top-$k$ candidate extraction. \crossnet{} solves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crossnet}
\min_{\Omega} \tfrac{1}{2}\|T - H_p\,\Omega\,H_q^{\!\top}\|_F^2 + \lambda_1\|\Omega\|_1 + \tfrac{\lambda_2}{2}\|\Omega - \Omega_{\mathrm{prior}}\|_F^2,
\end{equation}
where $H_p = I_p - \tfrac{1}{p}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\!\top}$ is the centering matrix. Eq.~\ref{eq:crossnet} is convex with smooth-plus-$\ell_1$ structure; \crossnet{} solves it via FISTA with adaptive momentum restart in $K\!\le\!20$ iterations of $O(pq)$ cost. The biological prior $\Omega_{\mathrm{prior}}$ encodes, for example, CRISPR-spacer-predicted lytic phage--host links as negative entries; setting $\lambda_2 = 0$ recovers the unprior'd estimator.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Single-domain: \fastprop{} vs.\ all five Case-A baselines}

We benchmarked \fastprop{} against five Case-A baselines on a shared planted-edge simulator: (i)~a from-scratch single-shot \sparcc{} (no bootstrap, no iterative exclusion -- the estimator isolated from the bootstrap cost); (ii)~Pearson correlation on CLR-transformed features; (iii)~Pearson on raw relative abundances; (iv)~\spieceasi{}-glasso~\cite{Kurtz2015} (graphical-lasso on CLR features); and (v)~\spieceasi{}-MB~\cite{Kurtz2015} (Meinshausen--Bühlmann neighborhood selection on CLR features). All six methods are scored by the same ranking metrics (AUROC, Recall@$K$) for apples-to-apples comparison (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_domain}b,c).

At $n\!=\!1{,}000$, $\rho\!=\!0.7$, AUROC against the planted-edge indicator is within $\pm 0.01$ for \fastprop{}, \sparcc{}, Pearson-on-CLR and \spieceasi{}-glasso at every $p \in \{500, 1{,}000, 2{,}000\}$ tested. \spieceasi{}-MB is $\sim 0.05$ AUROC lower than the marginal-correlation methods because it estimates partial correlations, which differ from the direct planted correlations by construction. Pearson on raw proportions is uniformly worst by $\sim 0.10$ AUROC at every $p \geq 500$. Crucially, \fastprop{}'s Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage produces a $\sim 3\times$ better Recall@$K$ at $p\!=\!1{,}000$ versus \sparcc{} and Pearson-on-CLR (0.30 vs.\ 0.10), because shrinkage cleanly separates the top-$K$ edges from the bulk.

\subsection{Speed: algorithmic vs.\ implementation acceleration}

\fastprop{} processes 5{,}000 OTUs across 500 samples in 0.42~s on a single CPU core using default NumPy BLAS, and scales as predicted by $O(np^2)$ to $p\!=\!10{,}000$, $n\!=\!2{,}000$ in 11~s (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_domain}d). On the same simulator our \sparcc{} Python implementation matches \fastprop{} within $\sim 1.5\times$ -- as expected, since both share the $O(np^2)$ inner loop. The $\sim 1{,}000\times$ gap re-emerges only when \sparcc{} is run as originally intended, with the $B\!\cdot\!I = 100\!\cdot\!10$ bootstrap-plus-iteration multiplier needed for per-pair significance. Our acceleration is therefore \emph{algorithmic} (eliminate the multiplier via analytic Fisher-$z$), distinct from \fastspar{}'s \emph{implementation} acceleration ($32\times$ single-thread, $221\times$ on 16 threads~\cite{Watts2019}). At $p \leq 2{,}000$ \fastprop{} is faster than \fastspar{} on any thread count; at $p \geq 5{,}000$, 16-thread \fastspar{} approaches \fastprop{} on raw wall-clock as the C++ constant catches up with our 1{,}000-fold operation-count reduction. The two accelerations are independent and would multiply in a C++ port. Importantly, neither \fastspar{} nor \spieceasi{} extends to the cross-domain problem solved next.

\subsection{Cross-domain: \crossnet{} vs.\ all four Case-C baselines}

On a planted phage--bacteria simulator at $p\!=\!q\!=\!500$, $n\!=\!300$, with 50 planted interactions (70\% lytic, 30\% lysogenic; 10 replicates), we compared five methods: naive CLR cross-covariance, \sparxcc{} base and iterative~\cite{SparXCC2024}, \spieceasi{}-cross (joint graphical lasso on $[\mathrm{CLR}_X, \mathrm{CLR}_Y]$ with the $p\!\times\!q$ cross block extracted), and \crossnet{} (Table~\ref{tab:cross_domain}). \crossnet{} differentiates on specificity: 0.849 versus 0.798 for \sparxcc{} -- a $\sim 25\%$ relative reduction in false-positive rate (0.202 $\to$ 0.151; Fig.~\ref{fig:cross_domain}c). The cost is $\sim 5$ percentage points of sensitivity, a precision--recall trade-off controlled by $\lambda_1$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:crossnet}. The convex formulation has no failure mode at symmetric-around-zero correlations -- the regime in which \citet{SparXCC2024} report iterative \sparxcc{} can perform \emph{worse} than the base estimator -- because the $\ell_1$ penalty replaces the iterative subset-selection step.

\section{Discussion}

\falcon{} extends the SparCC family in two orthogonal directions. First, its single-domain core (\fastprop{}) matches the AUROC of every standard Case-A method tested -- \sparcc{}, \spieceasi{}-glasso, and Pearson-on-CLR -- while trading the bootstrap-plus-iteration multiplier for an analytic Fisher-$z$ test, an \emph{algorithmic} acceleration that complements \fastspar{}'s \emph{implementation} acceleration~\cite{Watts2019}. Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage gives \fastprop{} a $\sim 3\times$ Recall@$K$ advantage at $p\!=\!1{,}000$ over its closest competitors. Second, \crossnet{} converts \sparxcc{}'s heuristic per-pair iterative correction into a convex matrix-level inverse problem with a closed-form formulation, provable convergence, and a built-in mechanism for signed biological priors. The convex formulation has no failure mode at symmetric-around-zero correlations that the iterative methods are known to degrade on. The precision--recall trade-off configurable through $\lambda_1$ allows users to tune \crossnet{} from a low-FPR setting suitable for expensive-to-validate phage--bacteria edges to a high-sensitivity setting matching \sparxcc{}.

The natural next step is application to paired phage--bacteria cohorts with CRISPR-spacer-derived priors, where the prior term $\Omega_{\mathrm{prior}}$ becomes operationally useful.
